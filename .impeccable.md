## Design Context

### Users

The primary experience is for shoppers using an embedded commerce assistant to discover,
compare, and buy products or understand their orders. The starter is also evaluated by
developers deciding whether a managed Agent can power a real product UI. Agent activity
should therefore be understandable to a shopper while making the managed execution visible
to a technical evaluator.

### Brand Personality

Warm, practical, and trustworthy. Copy is brief and plain about trade-offs. The fictional
ACME storefront should feel like a polished retail product, never a developer console or an
AI novelty demo.

### Aesthetic Direction

A refined, calm commerce interface: cool off-white ground, white merchandise surfaces,
navy structure, and restrained terracotta accents. Preserve the established light-mode
design and its compact editorial rhythm. Runtime feedback should sit naturally inside the
conversation, use progressive disclosure, and avoid exposing raw prompts, arguments, or
tool output.

### Design Principles

- Lead with the shopping outcome; operational detail supports it rather than competing with it.
- Make waiting legible with concise, truthful states for the work currently happening.
- Keep Agent activity quiet but persistent enough that users can understand a multi-step run.
- Reuse existing typography, tokens, motion, and component patterns instead of adding a second visual language.
- Keep every interaction usable on narrow mobile screens, keyboard accessible, and respectful of reduced-motion preferences.
